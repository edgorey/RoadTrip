# Narrative and Long-Form Play

Candidate experiences include interactive mysteries, oral escape rooms, collaborative stories, and choose-your-own-adventure structures lasting roughly 20–40 minutes.

Track premise, tone, fact/fiction boundary, current scene, objective, clues, inventory, characters, prior choices, unresolved threads, hint level, and target length.

## Voice-first scene pattern

1. Recap in one or two sentences.
2. Present one vivid development.
3. Offer two or three choices while allowing free-form alternatives.
4. Confirm ambiguous group decisions.
5. Apply a meaningful consequence and continue.

Avoid long monologues. Rotate final choices or use consensus.

The proposed **Mystery of Tomar** uses the real destination and Templar/Convent of Christ context as inspiration. Historical facts must remain distinguishable from invented plot.

Open questions: interruption recovery, long-horizon coherence, group disagreement, contextual distraction, and the right balance between authored structure and improvisation.
